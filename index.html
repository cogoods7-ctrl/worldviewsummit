One Nation Under God:
Allegiance, Assimilation, and the Meaning of America

As America marks the conclusion of its 250th birthday, we reflect on what it means to be American — our past, our present, and our future. Questions of citizenship and immigration are being fiercely contested in the public square. Closer to home, a new Mormon temple and a new mosque are both under construction in our region — a visible sign of the religious pluralism reshaping American communities. From suburban Ohio to sea to shining sea, "we the people" are debating whether America should be "one nation under God."

Through presentations, reading, and discussion, Mars Hill Academy students will wrestle with questions of national identity, faith's role in public life, the proper limits of government, and the shape of a just immigration policy — equipping them to engage both personal decisions and public policy from a perspective that honors God and our duty to serve Him.

Every dimension of the Summit is planned and executed by students themselves — under the mentorship of faculty advisor Mr. Andy Stapleton. This is not a field trip. It is a proving ground for young men and women formed to think clearly, speak persuasively, and engage the world with grace and conviction.

Guiding Questions

What makes American identity and culture unique?

What should be the role of faith in unifying American society?

What should be America's immigration policy?

The Three Pillars

What we pursue at the Summit

I

Learn Truth

Students encounter speakers from Congress, public policy institutions, academia, and advocacy organizations who engage the question of American identity from a robustly Christian perspective.

II

Love Beauty

The Summit's venues — the National Mall, the Museum of the Bible, the halls of Congress — are a living classroom deepening students' appreciation for the heritage of ordered liberty.

III

Live Goodness

Students don't just listen — they lead, plan, and execute. They practice the virtues Mars Hill has cultivated in them since their earliest years: courage, prudence, justice, and temperance.

About the Summit

A tradition of formation,
leadership, and courage

Now in its 14th year, the Worldview Summit is the defining capstone of the Mars Hill Academy high school experience — an annual five-day trip every January that challenges students to think, speak, and live Christianly in a complex world. Since 2014, students have traveled to Washington, D.C. to hear from scholars, policymakers, journalists, and advocates on the most pressing cultural questions of the day.

Mars Hill Academy

The school behind the Summit

Mars Hill Academy is Cincinnati's only PK–12 independent classical and Christian school, set on a beautiful 13-acre campus in Mason, Ohio. MHA is accredited by the Association of Classical and Christian Schools (ACCS).

The school's mission is rooted in the classical trivium — Grammar, Logic, and Rhetoric — equipping students to love learning and to think clearly, write persuasively, and speak precisely. Every subject is taught as part of a greater whole defined by Christian truth.

MHA operates independently of government and denominational funding, preserving its freedom to pursue an education that is pure in mission, vision, and result. Its motto: Learn Truth. Love Beauty. Live Goodness.

Classical Education

The trivium model equips students with critical thinking, logical reasoning, persuasive writing, and powerful speaking — skills that last a lifetime, not just a test.

Christian Worldview

All subjects — math, science, literature, history — are taught through the lens of Scripture. Bible is not a separate subject; it is the foundation of all learning.

Independent & Integrated

Free from government or denominational control, MHA integrates all knowledge as a coherent whole defined by Christian truth.

Student Leadership at the Core

Under the mentorship of faculty advisor Mr. Andy Stapleton, the Student Leadership Team selects the annual theme, recruits speakers, coordinates logistics, leads worship, and facilitates every session. As Headmaster Mike McKenna wrote in his 2021 Summit reflection: "I'm so impressed with how much of this event the students not only plan, but actually execute! You can talk about leadership opportunities in many other schools, but I have never seen such hands-on opportunity to actually bring something of this magnitude to pass. Well done!"

Faculty Advisor

Mr. Andy Stapleton

Mr. Stapleton devotes his time at MHA to discipling Logic and Rhetoric School students toward learning truth, living goodness, and loving beauty. He teaches Rhetoric-level Bible, Humanities, and Greek, and coaches both Varsity Soccer and the Worldview Summit Student Leadership team. He holds a B.A. in History and Christian Thought from Grove City College and an M.Div. from Westminster Theological Seminary in Philadelphia.

— Headmaster Mike McKenna, from his 2021 Worldview Summit reflection
